The Emerging Right to Longevity

The article's conclusion

  • Building blocks already exist in the edifice of human rights law: states' obligations to strive for the highest attainable standard of health (Art. 12 ICESCR) and to share the benefits of scientific progress (Art. 15 ICESCR).
  • As scientific reality extends what lifespan is attainable, implementation of existing rights must keep pace — an implicit (non-absolute) right to longevity is arguably already embedded in human rights instruments.
  • A person with the biological potential for a long, healthy life arguably has a right to the healthcare and technology that enable it.
  • Any advocacy for an international right to life extension must integrate the parallel frameworks — climate/biodiversity/e-waste regimes — to be credible and sustainable.

Recommendations

  1. Interpret the rights to health, life, and science to progressively cover effective longevity interventions as they mature.
  2. Pair any emerging right to longevity with an obligation of equitable access (SDG 10, COVAX-style distribution).
  3. Build international partnerships (SDG 17) between HICs and LMICs — public, private, and civil society — for equitable, inclusive AI longevity.
  4. Couple life-extension with sustainability (climate, biodiversity, e-waste guardrails).
  5. Keep the technology's purpose anchored in humanity's well-being: healthy, meaningful, potentially longer lives within a shared responsibility to each other and to future generations.
