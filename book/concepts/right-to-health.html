The Right to Health & Longevity

Article 12 ICESCR (right to health; also WHO Constitution) affirms entitlement to the "highest attainable standard of health" — traditionally interpreted as access to health services, sanitation, nutrition, and disease treatment. Two threshold questions for AI longevity:

  1. Can aging be a "treatable condition" under Article 12(2)(c) ("prevention, treatment and control of diseases")? — The article notes aging is not a pathological process but a normal human attribute; longevity is "a privilege that we all hope to enjoy."
  2. Are AI-based longevity tools "essential" medicines/services under Article 12(2)(d)? — Drafted around the WHO Essential Drugs list; many AI longevity interventions are still experimental, costly, and arguably "cosmetic" in nature, so they may not yet meet the "essential" threshold.

If they are essential

  • A longevity intervention deemed essential would become a global public good (analogy: vaccines in public-health emergencies), and UHC (SDG 3.8) would require equitable access.

AAAQ obligations

The right to health demands states ensure availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality. AI longevity tools are more readily available in high-income countries — risking widened health inequities between/within the Global North and South. States must not over-prioritize expensive curative AI therapies over broader public-health needs; the progressive-obligation standard applies given limited resources.
