The Longevity Divide

Core risk: life extension becoming another stark divider between rich and poor — a "two-tier humanity" of the long-lived and the left behind.

The disparities

  • Geographic: high-income countries (Japan, Italy, Switzerland) exceed 80-year life expectancies; many low/middle-income countries — especially sub-Saharan Africa — are below 60.
  • Demographic shift: by 2050, 80% of older people will live in LMICs, while the benefits of AI longevity would flow first to those with access.
  • Tech concentration: AI development/deployment is concentrated in the US and China, limiting LMIC access and raising questions of global health priorities and resource allocation.
  • Within-country: AI longevity tools are more available in the Global North and among the affluent; the "longevity divide" could exacerbate existing socioeconomic and geographic inequities.

Equity response (per the article)

  • SDG 10 + "leave no one behind": make sure life extension doesn't widen inequality; if LMICs gain access to AI longevity tools, they can accelerate their own life-expectancy gains.
  • COVAX model: the WHO vaccine-distribution mechanism as the template for allocating expensive, scarce longevity interventions fairly.
  • Representative data + age-inclusive co-creation (right to science) so older people and LMIC populations aren't excluded from the AI development process.
  • Intergenerational fairness: socioeconomic rights — pensions, flexible retirement, family-care burdens.
