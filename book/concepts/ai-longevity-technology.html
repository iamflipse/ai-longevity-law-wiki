AI & Longevity Technology

WHO context

  • WHO declared 2021–2030 the "Decade of Healthy Ageing", shifting healthy aging from "absence of disease" to maintenance of intrinsic capacity (physical, mental, social well-being).
  • ICD-11 withdrew "old age" as a diagnosis, replacing it with "aging-related" and "aging-associated decline in intrinsic capacity."
  • Demographics: the 60+ population will more than double to 2.1 billion by 2050; by 2100 nearly 1 in 3 people globally will be over 60; in 2020, 60+ people first outnumbered under-5s. Global life expectancy grew from ~32 years (1900) to 71+ years (2021).

AI's role in geroscience

  • Pattern-recognition over massive biomedical datasets to identify molecular drivers of aging and potential interventions.
  • Machine learning discovers drug candidates that clear senescent cells (AI-screened senolytics, e.g. Nature Comms 2023) or activate longevity-associated genes — screening billions of molecules in minutes.
  • "Aging clocks": AI-driven analysis of DNA, blood markers, and imaging to estimate biological age more accurately than chronological age (cross-link: aging-hallmarks wiki).
  • Biotechnologies: gene editing, stem-cell regenerative medicine, senolytics, epigenetic reprogramming (Harvard 2023: functional tissue rejuvenation in mice via epigenetic-marker alteration).
  • Realistic near-term impact: extending healthspan (+5–15 quality-adjusted years) rather than pushing maximum lifespan — which would require a major AI breakthrough in slowing the biological rate of aging.
  • Transhumanist horizon: AI + geroscience could foreseeably enable routine lifespans of 120 years and beyond.

The equity problem (preview)

Life expectancy gaps: high-income countries (Japan, Italy, Switzerland >80 y) vs. many LMICs (sub-Saharan Africa <60 y). 80% of older people will live in LMICs by 2050, while AI development is concentrated in the US and China — setting up the longevity divide.
