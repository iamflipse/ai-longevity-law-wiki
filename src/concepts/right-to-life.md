# The Right to Life, Extended

**Article 6 ICCPR, Article 3 UDHR** — the right to life has evolved from protection against *arbitrary* death to a duty to **sustain life with dignity**.

## The key question

Does the duty to protect life extend to an obligation to **"extend" lives** via AI longevity interventions — i.e., must states provide access and not withhold them?

## Key authorities

- **1982 Human Rights Committee:** the right to life "should not be interpreted narrowly"; states should adopt positive measures to **increase life expectancy** (eliminate malnutrition, epidemics) — explicitly linking lifespan extension to the right to life.
- But: the right to life concerns protection from **unnatural or premature** death, not immortality; it does **not** create a correlative obligation *to live* (that would clash with autonomy).
- **ECHR / UK / Australia:** states enjoy a **margin of appreciation** on life-sustaining treatment — no absolute duty to prolong life at all costs; natural death remains an inevitability, not an arbitrary deprivation.
- Net position: states must act "as expeditiously and effectively as possible" to progressively fulfil the right to life despite limited resources.
