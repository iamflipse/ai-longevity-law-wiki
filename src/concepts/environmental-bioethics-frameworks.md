# Environmental & Bioethics Frameworks

Longevity tech doesn't exist in a legal vacuum — several other international regimes condition how extended lifespans can be pursued responsibly:

## Climate

- **UNFCCC + Paris Agreement:** obligations to combat climate change while respecting human-rights duties, including the right to health (Paris preamble).
- **UN Human Rights Council (2021):** recognized the **right to a healthy environment**.
- Longer lives + AI data-center carbon footprint → states must balance demographic change with emissions targets; couple life-extension with sustainability measures.

## Biodiversity & biotech

- **CBD (1992) + Cartagena Protocol (2000):** biosafety rules for engineered organisms (engineered bacteria producing longevity drugs, viral gene-therapy vectors) — avoid adverse biodiversity effects and new health risks.
- **Nagoya Protocol (2010):** access & **benefit-sharing** — if AI finds a longevity molecule in an endemic plant, the biodiversity-rich country of origin is entitled to fair, equitable sharing of the scientific and economic benefits.

## E-waste

- **Basel Convention (1989):** controls transboundary movement of hazardous waste incl. **e-waste** from AI hardware, medical devices, and wearable health trackers; prevents dumping obsolete devices in developing countries; toxic components (mercury, lead) interact with the right to health.

## Bioethics

- **UNESCO Universal Declaration on Bioethics & Human Rights (2005, non-binding):** Articles 3 & 13 (informed consent, equality, international solidarity); **Article 16** — life sciences must not compromise future generations' well-being; **Article 17** — protect environment, biosphere, and biodiversity alongside biomedical advances.

**Bottom line:** these frameworks don't create a right to longevity but set **guardrails**: safety, consent, intergenerational justice.
