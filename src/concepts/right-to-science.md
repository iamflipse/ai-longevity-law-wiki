# The Right to Benefit from Scientific Progress

**Article 15(1)(b) ICESCR** — "the right of everyone to share in the benefits of scientific progress and its applications" (the "right to science").

- **General Comment 25** acknowledges emerging technologies (AI, biotechnology) enabling cures/treatments.
- **UNESCO Declaration on the Responsibilities of Present Generations towards Future Generations (1997)** and the **Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights (2005)**: scientific advancement must be shared equitably and used for human welfare — avoid a [longevity divide](longevity-divide.md).

## Data representativeness problem

- Most AI training datasets come from **high-income countries**, underrepresenting LMIC populations.
- **Older people** — the very population that would benefit most from longevity tech — are often absent from datasets and the co-creation process, risking unilateral exclusion and perpetuating ageist stigmatization.
- States should mandate **human-rights due diligence** for companies (representative + age-inclusive datasets) and a **lifecycle engagement** approach across AI development.

## Intellectual property tension

AI longevity tech is protected by IP systems, but IP "shall not be a detriment" to the right to science and health — a balance must be struck between IP protection and open sharing of scientific knowledge.
