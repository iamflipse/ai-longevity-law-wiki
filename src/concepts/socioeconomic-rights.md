# Socioeconomic Rights: Work, Pensions & Families

Longer, healthier lives stress the socioeconomic rights that underpin late-life security:

## Right to work (Article 6 ICESCR)

- Originally drafted to abolish forced labor; today underpins the **freedom to work** for personal development and socioeconomic inclusion.
- With longer working lives, states' non-discrimination duties should cover **older-age workers**: labor policies that empower older people, **flexible retirement** (age, timing, speed of labor-market exit), while balancing intergenerational opportunity so younger cohorts aren't displaced.

## Right to social security (Article 9 ICESCR)

- Guarantees dignity in old age, unemployment, and unaffordable healthcare.
- AI-enabled longevity grows reliance on public pensions while the **contributing working-age population shrinks** → states must set appropriate retirement ages and ensure pension sustainability across formal/informal economies, with **gender-sensitive adjustments** (women's caregiving roles + longer life expectancy).

## Family support (Article 10(1) ICESCR; Vienna IOPA Recommendations 25 & 29)

- States shall support families caring for dependent older members, especially low-income households.
- AI longevity tech may *reduce* dependence, but longer lives still impose **care burdens** on the younger generation's education/employment; family-caregiving (disproportionately women) must scale with longevity gains.
